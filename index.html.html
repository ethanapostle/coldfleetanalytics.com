Fleet Equipment — Lifecycle Costing & TCO

A guide for fleet managers on total cost of ownership, plus an interactive calculator

Fleet Management Guide

The sticker price is only the beginning. A reefer unit that costs $2,000 less at purchase can easily cost $15,000 more over 15 years once fuel burn, maintenance intervals, brand-specific wear parts, and labor rates are fully accounted for. This guide explains how to use the lifecycle cost calculator below to make that comparison with your own numbers.

What the calculator models

The calculator computes total cost of ownership (TCO) for Carrier and ThermoKing reefer units over your full operating period — up to 20 years. It accounts for six cost categories:

Capital costPer-unit purchase price, felt in year one and compared directly between brands.
MaintenanceEvery scheduled service event — parts cost plus technician labor (SRT × your shop rate) — triggered by operating hours.
FuelBurn rate ($/hr) × annual hours × years. Even a $0.14/hr difference compounds to tens of thousands across a large fleet.
TelematicsHardware installation cost plus monthly subscription, modeled across the full period.
Changeover costsYear-1 costs for switching to Carrier (tooling, training, inventory). The model tapers these over years 2–4.
Brand-specific eventsParts unique to one platform (nylon gear, head gaskets, generator, etc.) are marked Carrier-only or TK-only so comparisons stay apples-to-apples.

How to use it — four steps

  1. Fleet inputs

    Enter your fleet size, the percentage of Carrier units, average annual operating hours, and the number of years to model. Then fill in per-unit costs for each brand: capital, labor rate, fuel cost, telematics hardware and monthly fee, and Carrier changeover cost.

  2. Maintenance schedule

    Every service event is listed with editable fields for interval (hours), parts cost, and SRT (standard repair time in hours) for each brand. Change any value to match your actual negotiated pricing. Use the brand dropdown to flag Carrier-only or TK-only events — irrelevant cost fields grey out automatically. Use the + Add custom event button for anything not already in the list, and the × button to remove events that don't apply to your fleet.

  3. TCO summary

    Click Calculate TCO. The summary shows which brand wins, the per-unit savings amount, and the crossover year — the point where cumulative costs flip. A breakdown table separates capital, maintenance, fuel, telematics, and changeover so you can see exactly where each brand gains or loses. Fleet-level totals scale everything up to your full unit counts.

  4. Year-by-year view

    The year-by-year tab plots cumulative TCO for both brands on a line chart and shows annual and cumulative cost per year in a table. Use this to identify peak-cost years (where multiple major events converge) and to determine the minimum hold period required to realize the value of the higher-priced unit.

Tips for accurate results

Use negotiated parts pricesThe TK discount field handles % off list automatically. Enter your real contract pricing for Carrier parts.
Use your fully-loaded labor rateInclude overhead, not just the technician wage. A $100/hr vs $130/hr difference shifts the maintenance comparison significantly.
Run to full asset life12–15 years minimum. Short horizons favour the cheaper unit because capital cost dominates early and maintenance compounds later.
Add custom eventsAny fleet-specific rebuild or scheduled item not in the default list should be added — unmodelled events understate one brand's true cost.

Default values are loaded from a 200-asset Carrier fleet analysis and can be overridden completely. They are a starting point, not a recommendation.
